Watch it think

Your camera, the hand it finds, and what it decides that hand means — the same trained model the desktop app runs.

This can't control your computer, and won't pretend to. Nothing on this page can move your cursor, press a key or touch your volume — browsers don't grant pages that access. What you get is the recognizer's own view: the panel names the mode it would be in if the desktop app were listening. Your video is processed here, in this tab, and never uploaded — there's no server to upload it to.

This needs your camera. Only this tab sees it.

Do thisRecognized asDesktop app would
Point your index finger up, thumb tucked Cursor mode Move the pointer
Touch thumb to index fingertip, hold briefly, release Left click Click
Touch middle fingertip to index fingertip, hold, release Right click Right click
Closed fist, then move your hand up or down Scroll Scroll the active window
Thumb up, index down, move up or down Volume Change system volume
Pinch and hold, then move, then release Drag Press, drag, and drop
Closed fist, then flick left or right Swipe Switch desktop (rebindable)
Index up, thumb out sideways; spread or close them Zoom Zoom in or out
Index and pinky up, middle and ring down, hold 1.5 s Pause Switch all control off — and on again
L-shape, open palm, or two fingers A trained pose Fire the bound shortcut

The three trained poses are whatever's in the shipped model — which I trained on my own hands, in one room, under one lamp. It will almost certainly read your hands worse than it reads mine. That's not modesty, it's how the dataset was collected; the guide shows you how to retrain it on yourself.

Hand landmarks by MediaPipe Tasks, running locally via WebAssembly. Nothing here leaves the tab.
